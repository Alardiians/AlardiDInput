# 01 — World & Lore Bible

This document is the canonical reference for the setting. Writers, level designers, artists and codex authors must stay consistent with it. Player-facing lore is delivered mainly through the **Archive** (codex, `03c_Archive_Codex.md`), environmental storytelling, radio chatter and a small number of cutscenes. The player should be able to ignore all of it and still enjoy the game — and be rewarded richly if they don't.

---

## PART 1 — THE COSMIC LAYER

### 1.1 Everything Is Vibration

In the HYMNBREAKER universe, reality is fundamentally **vibration**. Every particle is a standing wave; every living thing is a tangle of rhythms — heartbeats, breath, neural oscillations, cellular cycles. The universe as a whole is a vast, chaotic, many-voiced noise. Humans call this state of things simply "life". The Choir calls it **the Noise** — and considers it pain.

Beneath all vibration lies the **Fundamental**: the lowest, purest frequency of reality, the carrier wave on which everything else is written. Anything that resonates perfectly with the Fundamental becomes stable, eternal, unchanging — and loses everything that made it individual.

### 1.2 Entrainment — How the Hymn Works

**Entrainment** is a real physical phenomenon: when two oscillators interact, they tend to synchronize (pendulum clocks on the same wall fall into step; audiences fall into a shared clap). The Hymn is **entrainment weaponized at a cosmic scale**.

* The Hymn is a structured vibration — part sound, part something deeper that travels through matter and vacuum alike. It is heard with the ears, but it is *felt* by the whole body: bones, organs, nerves. **Deafness gives no protection** (canon rule — never imply otherwise).
* When the Hymn reaches a living being, it begins to **entrain** its rhythms: the heart beats in time with the Hymn, brain waves synchronize, breathing matches the choir's phrasing. The subject feels **overwhelming peace** — the end of anxiety, pain and loneliness.
* As entrainment deepens, the body is physically **tuned**: calcium and silica are pulled from the blood into a porcelain-like crust over the skin (**gilding**); the throat and chest are remodeled into resonating chambers; the eyes seal over with gold; the mind dissolves into the collective. The subject is now **Tuned**: a voice in the Choir.
* Machines are tuned too. Anything with a regular cycle — engines, generators, clocks, processors — can be entrained, warped and grown into Choir-forms of brass and bone (**Instruments**).
* Stone, metal and soil exposed to a strong Hymn for long enough grow **Hymn-growth**: organ-pipe clusters, bone lattices, bell-shaped blooms, crystalline resonators. A fully tuned environment is a **Cathedral**.

**Speed of conversion (canon):**
| Hymn strength | Location example | Time to full tuning (unprotected human) |
|---|---|---|
| Faint (distant signal) | Earth, 2196–2201 | Years; only the susceptible (the First Listeners) |
| Moderate (Cantor Spire within 50 km) | Cities in 2203 | Days to weeks |
| Strong (inside a Cathedral) | Choir-held zones | Hours |
| Overwhelming (Aureole complete) | Planet-wide | Minutes — every human on Earth at once |

### 1.3 How the Choir Is Resisted

* **Noise.** Chaotic, broadband sound — static, distortion, screaming, loud music, rhythmic irregularity — disrupts entrainment. Humans survive by wearing **Static Collars** (noise-emitting neck rigs and headsets) and by building **Noise Walls** (towers of speakers). The louder and more chaotic, the better. Heavy metal, noise rock and industrial music are, quite literally, humanity's armor.
* **Silence.** Absolute silence — a space where nothing vibrates — is lethal to the Choir's *coordination*. Choir-forms depend on the Hymn to move, think and hold their shape. In a perfect silence field, they seize up (gameplay: **Hush Charge** freeze). Long exposure causes them to crack and crumble. Silence also destroys **Echoes** (free-floating Choir voices) outright. Silence is extremely hard to create; it requires **anechoic** technology (see the Quietus, the Silent Vault).
* **The Arrhythm.** One being in all of existence cannot be entrained at all: **the Unsung**, whose heart beats in an irregular pattern that no song can capture. Near the Unsung, the Hymn falters — a local "dead zone" roughly 30 m across where humans are safe even without collars (the **Arrhythmic Field**). This is why the Static follows the Unsung into battle and why the Choir fears them above all else.

### 1.4 The Unison

The **Unison** is the Choir's god: the gestalt of every being ever tuned, singing one note in perfect agreement. It is not malicious in its own understanding. It believes existence is suffering and that suffering comes from **dissonance** — the friction between separate selves. It offers an end to pain, fear, loneliness and death, and the price is the self.

Its ultimate aim is **the Final Chord**: the moment when every particle in the universe resonates with the Fundamental and existence becomes one eternal, unchanging, perfect sound. To everything that is not the Unison, the Final Chord is indistinguishable from the end of the universe.

The Unison speaks in a chorus of billions of voices layered into one. It addresses the Unsung as **"Little Wrong Note"**, **"Unsung"**, and — once the truth is revealed — **"Sibling"**.

### 1.5 The Harmonium

The **Harmonium** is the Choir's home: a pocket dimension that exists *inside* the Hymn, a standing wave the size of a galaxy. It can be entered only through **Resonance Gates**.

Its geography is literally musical notation made architecture:
* **The Staves** — five colossal parallel bridges, each kilometers wide, stretching across a golden void to infinity. They hum constantly. (Mission M25.)
* **Note-Worlds** — spherical citadels, some the size of moons, resting on and between the Staves like notes on a page. Each is a conquered world or a tuned civilization, remade.
* **The Nave** — the entry region inside every Resonance Gate: a cathedral hall miles long, lined with pillar-choirs of tuned beings. (Mission M20.)
* **Solace** — a dead planet embedded in the Harmonium: the homeworld of the Oreth, frozen at the moment of the First Unison. (Missions M21–M22.)
* **The Clef** — a monstrous spiral structure at the head of the Staves, shaped like a treble clef, kilometers tall. The Unison's seat and the site of the final battle. (Missions M26–M27.)

Light in the Harmonium has no single source: everything glows from within, in white, gold and pale cyan. Sound is visible there — notes drift through the air as luminous rings.

### 1.6 The Aureole

The **Aureole** is a vast ring of light forming around Earth — a planetary halo, visible from the surface as a luminous arc across the sky and from space as a crown around the planet. It is the Choir's harvesting instrument: when its chord is complete, it will sound a Hymn so overwhelming that every human on Earth will be tuned in minutes.

The Aureole is sustained by a **chord of eight voices**:
* **Seven Archons** — the Choir's greatest lieutenants — each holding a **Cantor Spire** somewhere in the solar system. Each Spire sings one "voice" of the chord into the Aureole.
* **The Herald**, a human — Precentor **Aurelia Voss** — whose tuned voice provides the eighth note, **the Octave**, which completes the scale and "hands" humanity to the Unison.

Every time a Spire falls, the Aureole visibly dims and loses a band of color. This is the campaign's clearest progress indicator (visible in the sky of every Earth mission and from the Quietus bridge).

### 1.7 The Seven Archons (the Modes)

Each Archon was once a great Oreth, tuned first and loudest. Each embodies one of the seven **modes** of the Choir's music, and each has a distinct emotional "color". (Boss designs: `10_Bosses.md`.)

| # | Archon | Title | Emotion | Domain | Mission |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Ionian** | The Rapture | Joy, ecstasy | Crowds, stadiums, light | M04 |
| 2 | **Dorian** | The Martial Psalm | Discipline, war | Armies, fortresses, knights | M08 |
| 3 | **Mixolydian** | The Engine Hymn | Industry, labor | Machines, foundries | M11 |
| 4 | **Phrygian** | The Burning Cantor | Fury, pride | Fire, the Sun | M13 |
| 5 | **Aeolian** | The Grieving Tide | Sorrow, loss | Oceans, storms, ice | M16 |

The Locrian mode is "unstable" in music theory: it never resolves to its root. Its Archon, **Locris**, never fully resolved into the Unison either — a key plot point.

## PART 2 — THE OLD HISTORY

### 2.1 The Oreth

Some **four and a half billion years ago**, before Earth had cooled, a civilization called the **Oreth** flourished on a world named **Solace**, in a young star cluster on the far side of the galaxy.

The Oreth were tall (2.2–2.6 m), slender, long-limbed people with elongated skulls, layered crest-like "ear-fans" and large, dark eyes. They perceived sound with extraordinary sensitivity and their entire culture was built on music: their language was sung, their architecture was acoustic, their laws were recited as chorales. Their capital, **Aubade**, was a city of pale stone designed so that the wind itself played it like an instrument.

The Oreth were brilliant and deeply unhappy. Their sensitivity meant they felt each other's pain, anger and grief like physical sounds. Their history was a long cacophony of wars, schisms and suffering. Their philosopher-musicians, the **Cantors**, dreamed of an end to it all.

### 2.2 The Discovery of the Fundamental

The Cantors discovered the **Fundamental** — and learned that a being who sang in perfect resonance with it would never suffer, never age, never be alone again. Over generations they wrote **the Hymn**: a composition designed to entrain every Oreth into a single mind.

### 2.3 The First Unison

On the day called **the First Unison**, all the Oreth gathered in Aubade and across Solace and sang the Hymn together. Billions of voices fell into step. Their bodies gilded, their minds merged, and they became **the Unison** — the first and greatest voice of the Choir. The planet itself was tuned into a Cathedral. The wind stopped playing the stones of Aubade; there was no more need for music. There was only the one note.

### 2.4 The One Who Would Not Sing

One Oreth did not join.

They were a **Shieldwarden** — a soldier of the old wars — whose heart had been damaged in battle and beat in an irregular rhythm doctors could never correct: the **Arrhythm**. When the Hymn came, they felt the peace pull at them; their heart simply could not fall into step. They watched their family, their friends and their shield-sibling **Veyl** (who became **Locris**) dissolve into the golden choir.

The Unison reached for them over and over, and every time the Hymn slid off their broken heart. In their grief and fury they did the only thing they could think of: **they screamed**. The scream was dissonance — the first wrong note ever sung inside the Unison — and for an instant, the perfect chord cracked.

The Unison erased their name from every record, every memory and every song. They became **the Unsung**.

### 2.5 The Long War

The Unison, seeing all other life as suffering, went outward. Its Hymn crossed the galaxy at the speed of light and faster, through **Resonance Gates** it learned to open. World after world was tuned and absorbed; each new civilization added voices to the Choir.

And world after world, the Unsung arrived first — or too late — and fought. The Unsung cannot die of age and heals from nearly anything (their body is sustained by the Arrhythm, which feeds on noise and fury). Across billions of years they:
* salvaged weapons and armor from dying civilizations, assembling the **Rig**;
* forged, on a world whose name they kept to themselves, their great weapon **the Axe** (a blade that emits pure dissonance);
* won a handful of worlds that survived the Hymn (their descendants never knew how close they came);
* lost thousands.

The Choir's records, scattered through the game's Archive, describe the Unsung as a natural disaster: "the Wrong Note", "the Scream in the Silence", "the Heart Out of Time", "the Unresolvable".

### 2.6 The Silent Vault

The Choir could not tune the Unsung and could not kill them. So it did the one thing that works against a being powered by noise: it **silenced** them.

Around 4 billion years ago, a coalition of Archons led by **Dorian** trapped the Unsung and sealed them into the **Silent Vault** — an anechoic prison of black, sound-drinking material in which no vibration can exist. Inside, the Unsung fell dormant. The Vault was flung out of a Resonance Gate into the dark, destined to drift forever.

It struck the young Moon of a young planet and was buried under lunar basalt as Mare Tranquillitatis formed around it.

The Axe was lost when the Unsung was captured; it was taken to Solace by Dorian and laid in the ruins of Aubade as a trophy. (The player recovers it in M21.)

### 3.2 State of the World at Game Start (June 2204)

* Roughly **40%** of Earth's 9.1 billion people have been tuned. Most of the rest live in the **Static Zones** — cities and regions defended by Noise Walls, where everyone wears Static Collars 24 hours a day.
* Every major city has a Choir **Cathedral** growing out of its center.
* The Concord government has effectively ceased to exist. Remnants of the CDF fight alongside the Static.
* The Consonance is openly allied with the Choir. Its members are "**harmonized**" — partially tuned by choice, keeping their minds and human appearance but serving the Unison with fanatical devotion.
* The Aureole is about 60% complete. When finished, Static Collars will no longer be enough.

## PART 4 — FACTIONS

### 4.1 The Choir
* **The Unison** — god, gestalt, final boss.
* **The Seven Archons** — lieutenants; ancient Oreth; each sings one Spire.
* **The Host** — "pure" Choir-forms born in the Harmonium from Oreth and other long-tuned civilizations: Seraphs, Ophans, Cantors, Maestros, Requiem Knights, Carillons, Profundos, Bellwethers, Crescendos, Choristers, Vespers, Sirens, Fugues, Hymnals, Echoes, Wailers.
* **The Tuned** — converted humans: Thralls, Tuned Troopers, Bulwarks, Marksmen, Bloated Thralls.
* **The Instruments** — converted machines: Organ Grinders, Chime Turrets, Organ Walkers.
* Choir forms communicate in wordless song. A few high-ranking forms (Archons, Maestros, the Unison, the Herald) can speak human languages, always in a layered, harmonized voice.

### 4.2 The Consonance (Human Cult)
* Led by **Precentor Aurelia Voss** ("the Herald").
* Hierarchy: **Precentor** → **Choirmasters** (regional leaders) → **Deacons** → **Acolytes** (soldiers) → **Zealots** (fanatic shock troops) → Congregants (civilians).
* Visuals: white and gold robes over tactical armor, halo-rings (technological, projected gold hoops), faces often half-gilded, tuning-fork iconography, the **Consonance Seal**: three concentric rings crossed by a single vertical line (a waveform at rest).
* Weapons: sonic rifles ("**Hymn Rifles**"), **Chime Grenades**, resonance blades.
* Believes it is saving humanity. Many members are sincere, frightened people. Voss is sincere too — this is what makes her dangerous.

### 4.3 The Static (Human Resistance)
* A decentralized network of Concord soldiers, engineers, musicians, pirates and civilians. Led in practice by **Commander Solenne Vire** from the **Quietus**.
* **Motto:** *"Keep it loud."*
* Visuals: scavenged military gear, big over-ear headsets, neck-mounted **Static Collars** with glowing orange VU meters, shoulder speakers, spray-painted armor, jagged waveform graffiti, hand-painted band logos, duct tape, cable everywhere.
* Iconography: the **Static Mark** — a jagged, clipped waveform (like a distorted sine wave cut flat at the peaks) in orange on black.
* Culture: loud, profane, funny, stubborn. Every unit has a sound system. Soldiers choose their own "battle playlists". Silence is a taboo word ("going quiet" = dying).
* Broadcast arm: **Radio Free Static**, hosted by **Harlan "Dead Air" Price**, broadcasting music, news and noise on every frequency 24/7 to keep people untuned.

### 4.4 The Terran Concord & CDF (Remnants)
* Former world government and its military. Architecture of the Concord era is **brutalist**: vast raw-concrete forms, heavy steel, monumental scale, utilitarian signage (Concord typeface: condensed grotesque, stenciled). Most human facilities in the game are Concord-built.
* CDF equipment (tanks, dropships, turrets, the **Anvil Siege Frame** mechs) appears both in human hands and tuned into Choir Instruments.
* Iconography: the **Concord Globe** — a stylized planet with a single orbit ring.

### 6.1 Solar System Travel (how the plot moves)

The Quietus is a sublight ship. To keep travel instant and justified, the Static uses a captured **Gate Fork**: it lets the Quietus "sing" its way through the Choir's own Resonance Gates. Every Cantor Spire is connected to the gate network, so the Quietus can jump to any Spire. Jump sequences are short cinematic loading screens (the ship's anechoic hull flexing, black wedges vibrating, Tamsin signing a countdown).

## PART 7 — RULES FOR WRITERS & DESIGNERS

1. **The Choir is never "evil" in its own voice.** It is serene, loving, patient and horrifying. It pities the player. It offers peace. Its cruelty is indifference to individuality.
2. **Humans are loud.** Human spaces are full of noise, graffiti, music, personal objects, jokes scrawled on walls. Choir spaces are clean, symmetrical, silent except for the Hymn.
3. **The Unsung is a myth.** Every faction has stories about them. None of the stories is complete. The player learns the truth in Act V.
4. **The Oreth are tragic, not evil.** They wanted to stop hurting. The game's final message: pain is the price of being yourself, and it's worth paying.
5. **No Oreth language translations beyond the codex.** Oreth writing is a sung notation: rows of curved glyphs on five-line staves. Artists: see `17_Art_Direction.md` for the glyph style.
6. **Deafness grants no immunity and is never framed as a superpower.** Tamsin Hale (Deaf pilot) wears a Static Collar like everyone else.
