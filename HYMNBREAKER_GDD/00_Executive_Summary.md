# 00 — Executive Summary

## 1. High Concept

**HYMNBREAKER** is a lightning-fast, ultra-violent first-person shooter in which a silent, ancient warrior fights a cosmic invasion of angelic horrors across the Moon, a dying Earth, the outer solar system and finally the singing dimension the invaders call home.

The enemy is not chaos. The enemy is **perfect harmony**. The **Hymn** is a song older than stars that "tunes" everything it touches — flesh, minds, machines, even stone — into a single collective choir. Converted beings become the **Choir**: porcelain-skinned, gold-veined, haloed monsters whose attacks are notes of light and whose architecture is grown from bone organ-pipes and bells.

The hero is **the Unsung**: the only being the Hymn has ever failed to sing. Their heart beats out of time. They are dissonance given a body — black iron, rust, embers and noise. When the Choir sings, the Unsung screams back.

**Logline:** *The universe is being sung into one perfect voice. You are the wrong note.*

**Tagline:** *BREAK THE HYMN.*

## 2. Design Pillars

### Pillar 1 — LOUDER THAN THE HYMN (Power Fantasy)
You are the apex predator. Enemies are terrifying, but the player is worse. Every weapon feels colossal; every kill is a spectacle; enemies flinch, crack, shatter and flee. The Choir's own dialogue treats you as an unstoppable natural disaster.
* *Test:* Would a first-time player grin after this encounter? If a fight makes the player feel weak for more than a few seconds, it is broken.

### Pillar 2 — MOVE OR BE SUNG (Push-Forward Combat)
Standing still is death. Health, ammo and armor come from **aggression**: executions give Health, the Harrow rips ammo out of enemies, Distort makes enemies bleed armor. The best defense is a violent offense. Movement (dash, double jump, Harrow grapple, Kick Pads, swing poles) is fast, precise and always available.
* *Test:* Can the player refill every resource without leaving the fight? They must.

### Pillar 3 — EVERY TOOL HAS A PURPOSE (Combat Chess at 200 BPM)
Each enemy has a readable weakness and each weapon has a role. Shields melt under the Static Rifle; an Ophan swallowing a Frag Slug drops out of the sky; a Seraph's wing-pipes can be sniped to ground it. Mastery means choosing the right tool in a fraction of a second, not memorizing a rotation.
* *Test:* Every enemy introduced must have (a) a weakness, (b) a clear audio-visual tell, (c) an intended "answer" weapon or ability.

### Pillar 4 — ARENAS AND ADVENTURE (Rhythm of Play)
Levels alternate between **locked combat arenas** (intense, 2–6 minutes) and **exploration/traversal** (secrets, lore, platforming, set pieces, breathers). Like a song: verse, chorus, verse, chorus, bridge, breakdown.
* *Test:* No more than ~8 minutes of pure exploration without combat; no more than ~3 back-to-back arenas without a breather.

### Pillar 5 — THE SONG OF WAR (Audio-Driven Design)
Sound is the theme, the story and a mechanic. The adaptive metal soundtrack follows the player's **Gain** meter. Enemies telegraph through sound (every enemy has a signature chord). Silence is a weapon (Hush Charges freeze the Choir). The finale is humanity's collective noise.
* *Test:* With the screen off, could an expert tell what is happening in a fight? They should be able to.

## 3. Unique Selling Points (what makes it not-DOOM)

| USP | Description |
|---|---|
| **Harmony vs. Distortion** | Inverted iconography: the monsters look like angels; the hero looks like a demon. The villain's goal is peace-through-erasure; the hero stands for the right to be loud, painful, individual and free. |
| **Gain → Overdrive** | A performance meter that literally drives the music. Aggression, variety and style raise Gain; at full Gain the player triggers **Overdrive** and the soundtrack kicks into a live guitar-solo layer while the Unsung becomes a berserk engine of destruction. |
| **The Harrow** | A chain-harpoon on the left arm that combines three verbs in one tool: **Shred** fodder into ammo, **grapple** to anchors, and **pull yourself** into heavy enemies. |
| **Counter (parry)** | Gold "Resonant Notes" can be punched back at the enemy. Precise, skill-based, and rewarded with Plating and Gain. |
| **Silence as a weapon** | The Choir needs sound to move. **Hush Charges** create bubbles of absolute silence that freeze them, and **Echoes** (possessing spirits) can only be destroyed by silence. |
| **Weak-point sandbox** | Destructible weak points on most heavies (bell-mouths, wing-pipes, turrets, lanterns) that change enemy behavior when destroyed. |
| **Porcelain gore** | Enemies crack like porcelain under fire, revealing red flesh and golden inner light, and burst into shards and gore — a distinctive gore language. |
| **The Pedalboard** | Perks are guitar-effects "Pedals" slotted onto a pedalboard. |
| **Radio Free Static** | A pirate-radio DJ narrates the war, introduces missions and plays the soundtrack — then organizes humanity's final weapon: the **Last Broadcast**. |
| **Set-piece variety** | Space-elevator descent, a boarded hub ship, underwater-pressure habitats on Europa, a floating city on Venus, a dead alien capital frozen mid-song, three **Anvil Siege Frame** mech segments. |

## 4. Player Experience Goals

* **Minute 1:** Silence. Absolute silence. Then a crack of static, a single down-tuned guitar chord — and the Unsung's eyes open.
* **Minute 5:** The player has torn through a room of converted humans with a shotgun, ripped one apart with the Harrow and watched ammo spill out.
* **Hour 1:** The player understands the resource triangle (Breakdown → Health, Shred → Ammo, Distort → Plating) and is already dashing between arenas instinctively.
* **Hour 5:** First Archon defeated in a stadium full of rapturous converts. The player owns 5 weapons and is weapon-switching mid-combo.
* **Hour 15:** The player is running 8+ weapons, mods, Pedals and Overdrive; encounters are complex puzzles solved at speed; the player has learned the Choir came to Earth because of *them*.
* **Hour 25:** The player walks through the dead city of the hero's own people. For once, the music stops.
* **Hour 34:** Eight billion humans scream into the dark at once, and the player carries that scream into the heart of god.

## 5. Target Audience & Comparables

**Primary:** Players 18–40 who loved *DOOM (2016)*, *DOOM Eternal*, *DOOM: The Dark Ages*.
**Secondary:** Retro/modern boomer-shooter fans (*Ultrakill*, *Dusk*, *Prodeus*, *Quake*), action fans (*Devil May Cry*, *Titanfall 2* campaign), metal music fans.

| Comparable | What we take | What we do differently |
|---|---|---|
| DOOM Eternal | Resource loop, weak points, arena design, hub, collectibles, mastery | Harrow tri-verb tool, parry, Gain-driven music, sonic enemy tells |
| DOOM (2016) | Brutal pacing, simpler readability, atmosphere | Stronger narrative presence via radio DJ and cast |
| DOOM: The Dark Ages | Parry weight, mech set pieces, big-scale battles | Parry integrated into melee, not a shield |
| Ultrakill | Style meter → rewards | Gain is simpler, feeds Overdrive and music, not a rank |
| Titanfall 2 | Mission variety, one-off set pieces | Mech segments are rarer and bigger |

## 7. Platform & Technical Targets (summary)

* **Engine:** Unreal Engine 5 (latest stable 5.x supported by the team's UE MCP; minimum 5.4).
* **Platforms:** PC (Windows) first. Architecture must not preclude consoles.
* **Performance:** 60 FPS locked at 1440p on an RTX 3070-class GPU (High preset, TSR/DLSS/FSR quality); 120 FPS mode on high-end; 30 FPS floor never acceptable in combat.
* **Rendering:** Nanite (static environment), Lumen GI & reflections (scalable to screen-space on Low), Virtual Shadow Maps, Niagara VFX, MetaSounds + Quartz for music sync.
* **Input:** Keyboard/mouse and gamepad, fully rebindable, via Enhanced Input.
* **Build approach:** C++ systems + data-driven content + thin Blueprints. Full detail in `20_Technical_Design_UE5.md`.

## 8. Tone

* **Brutal, loud, gleefully excessive** in combat.
* **Awe and dread** in the Choir's spaces: vast, beautiful, wrong.
* **Warm, funny, human** in the hub and on the radio. Humanity is scrappy, profane, loud and alive.
* **Tragic and mythic** in the Oreth revelations.
* Never cynical about the hero. The Unsung is never mocked, never loses dignity. Mild dark humor through hero *actions* (smashing a monitor while the villain monologues) is encouraged.
